Source 1 is the extract of Directive 2011/7/EU (Articles 1 to 7). Source 2 is the
owner's note, which records what the owner believes; it is not used as evidence.

1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: As **simple interest** for late payment at a rate equal to the **reference
   rate plus at least eight percentage points**. For a Member State whose currency is the
   euro, the reference rate is either the interest rate the European Central Bank applied
   to its most recent main refinancing operations, or the marginal interest rate from
   variable-rate tender procedures for those operations. For a Member State whose currency
   is not the euro, it is the equivalent rate set by its national central bank. The
   reference rate is fixed per half year: the rate in force on 1 January applies to the
   first half of the year, and the rate in force on 1 July applies to the second half.

   Sources: Source 1, Article 2(6) (definition); Article 2(7)(a)(i), 2(7)(a)(ii) and
   2(7)(b) (reference rate); Article 3(2) (half-year rule between businesses; Article 4(2)
   is the same rule for public authorities).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: **No.** The directive does not set a maximum late fee. It sets a **minimum**:
   where interest for late payment becomes payable, the creditor is entitled to obtain
   from the debtor, as a minimum, a fixed sum of **EUR 40** as compensation for its own
   recovery costs. On top of that fixed sum, the creditor is entitled to reasonable
   compensation for recovery costs above EUR 40 caused by the late payment (for example a
   lawyer or a debt collection agency). The sources give no figure of 25 EUR. Note also
   that the directive tells Member States what to ensure; the national law that applies
   to the business is not in the sources, so the exact wording for a notice is for the
   owner to confirm.

   Sources: Source 1, Article 6(1) (minimum fixed sum of EUR 40), Article 6(2) (it is
   compensation for recovery costs), Article 6(3) (further reasonable compensation).
   The 25 EUR claim is from Source 2, point 1.

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: **No.** Between businesses, the creditor is entitled to interest for late
   payment **without the necessity of a reminder**, if (a) it has fulfilled its own
   contractual and legal obligations and (b) it has not received the amount due on time,
   unless the debtor is not responsible for the delay. Interest runs from the day after
   the payment date or period fixed in the contract; if no date or period is fixed, it
   runs after 30 calendar days from receipt of the invoice (with variants when that date
   is uncertain or goods or services arrive later). The fixed EUR 40 sum is also payable
   **without the necessity of a reminder**. The same "no reminder" rule applies when the
   debtor is a public authority. So the owner's belief that interest needs a reminder
   first is not supported by the sources: reminders are a courtesy, not a condition.

   Sources: Source 1, Article 3(1), 3(1)(a) and 3(1)(b) (no reminder needed, conditions);
   Article 3(3)(a) and 3(3)(b) (from when); Article 6(1) and 6(2) (fixed sum, no reminder
   needed); Article 4(1) (public authorities). The belief is from Source 2, point 2.

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: **Not in the sources.** Source 1 only defines which rate counts as the
   reference rate (the ECB rate on its most recent main refinancing operations, or the
   marginal rate from variable-rate tenders, for euro countries); it gives no figure, and
   it is the text as adopted in 2011, so it could not give a current one. To answer, I
   would need the ECB's official publication of the main refinancing operations rate. For
   today's date (2026-10-12, second half of the year), the applicable value is the rate in
   force on **1 July 2026**. Also, putting this rate in the final notice would add a
   figure that is not in the invoice data, which the reminder rules currently forbid; that
   is the owner's decision.

   Sources: Source 1, Article 2(7)(a)(i) and 2(7)(a)(ii) (what the rate is) and
   Article 3(2)(b) (rate in force on 1 July applies to the second half of the year).

Additional note for the owner (Source 2, point 3, the 90-day term): between businesses,
the payment period fixed in the contract must not exceed 60 calendar days **unless
otherwise expressly agreed in the contract and provided it is not grossly unfair to the
creditor**. So a 90-day term is not automatically ruled out, but it must be expressly
agreed and not grossly unfair. Sources: Source 1, Article 3(5) and Article 7(1).
